The thing to know before you start breeding

Work-speed passives are universal — they apply to all twelve jobs equally, so one template covers every base Pal you will ever breed. The exception is Farming: it is the only job with passives that raise its work suitability level, and it is also the only job that needs them.

So the breeding problem is small: four templates cover the whole game — a general worker, a ranch worker, a combat Pal, and a mount. Everything else is just which Pal you stamp the template onto, because the job is decided by work suitability level, not by passives.

Max throughput — +245%

Demon’s Hand + Remarkable Craftsmanship + Artisan + Work Slave. The ceiling. Feed it well and keep a Hot Spring nearby, because Demon’s Hand burns sanity.

Round-the-clock — +215%

Swap Work Slave for Insomnia on any non-nocturnal Pal. You lose 30% speed but gain the whole night, which is a net win on anything running unattended.

Low-maintenance

Hermit Sage + Artisan + Serious. Less peak speed, far less micromanagement — the sanity and hunger drain stops forcing you back to base.

Never put this on a base Pal

Musclehead is −50% Work Speed. It is a combat passive (+30% Attack) and it is also sold as an implant at the Bounty Officer, so it is easy to apply to the wrong Pal by accident. Check before you spend the token.

Sanity is a food problem, not a passive problem

Demon’s Hand costs you SAN, but the fix is quality food and Hot Springs — not dropping the passive. Giving up 90% work speed to save a sanity slot is the wrong trade on a base you visit regularly. Hermit Sage is the right answer only for bases you genuinely leave alone for long stretches.

6. Ranch Pals — the one job with its own passives

Farming is the odd one out, in both directions: it has the lowest natural cap of any job, and it is the only job with passives that raise its suitability level.

PassiveEffectRank
Ranch MasterFarming’s Work Suitability +2Rank 4 (diamond)
FarmhandFarming’s Work Suitability +1Rank 3 (gold)

These are the only two passives in 1.0 that directly raise a work suitability, and both are Farming-only. Nothing equivalent exists for Handiwork, Mining, Kindling or any other job. Confirmed in-game.

Why Farming and nothing else

Most jobs cap at Farming 4 naturally, against 8 for the other suitabilities and 7 for Transporting. Farming starts four levels behind everything else, which is exactly why it gets dedicated passives and the others do not.

Stacking a ranch Pal to the ceiling

  1. Natural cap — Farming 4Where every ranch Pal starts.
  2. +2 Ranch Master → 6Rank-4 passive. The single biggest jump available.
  3. +1 Farmhand → 7Rank-3 passive. Two of your four slots are now spent, which is the real cost of this build.
  4. +1 Applied Ranching Handbook → 8300 Dog Coin at the Medal Merchant. Permanent, and it follows the Pal between bases. 1.0 added this one specifically for Farming.
  5. +1 Cinnamoth work aura → 9Station a Cinnamoth at the base: its partner skill gives +1 Farming to every other Pal there. Does not stack with duplicates, does not benefit itself, and cannot be improved by condensation. There are 12 such aura Pals, one per job — Ribbuny does the same for Handiwork.
  6. Condensation → 10Each rank-up raises one suitability; max rank raises them all. 48 Pals to max rank.
The ranch template

Ranch Master + Farmhand + two work-speed passives. Work speed raises ranch output, not just animation rate, so the last two slots want Artisan (buyable, 15 Bounty Tokens) plus Demon’s Hand, Remarkable Craftsmanship or Work Slave.

Both multipliers are real and independent: suitability level from the two Farming passives, and output from work speed. Plus condensation rank raising drop quantity per cycle on top.

Why this compounds faster than it looks

Your breeding programme is gated by Cake, Cake by Honey, and Honey has no vendor at any price — it comes off a Beegarde ranch and nowhere else. So a maxed Beegarde directly accelerates the pipeline that produces your passives.

Same for Sibelyx Primo (High Quality Cloth), Dumud (HQ Pal Oil → Polymer) and Flambelle (Flame Organ → Carbon Fiber). Those four ranch slots feed most of the endgame.

How to actually get these onto a Pal

  1. BreedingThe main route. Both parents pass passives down, so build up a stock of donor Pals carrying single good passives, then combine. Condense first, breed second — condensation rank is not inherited.
  2. Implants — the shortcutBuyable, no breeding required, and split across two currencies with no overlap. Bounty Officer, 15 tokens each: Artisan, Musclehead, Motivational Leader, Stronghold Strategist, Vanguard, Burly Body, Wellness Watcher. Arena, 50 Battle Tickets each: Serenity, Runner, Ace Swimmer, Infinite Stamina, Noble, Reload Master, Healing Coach.
  3. Surgery tableTransfers passives between Pals. Not every passive is transferable — check before you plan a build around it.
  4. Pal ReverserRerolls passives. 7 Bounty Tokens, or 35 Battle Tickets. Cheap enough to fish for a fourth slot.
  5. Mutated eggsThe only source of Rainbow passives. Not breedable on demand.
The efficient order

Artisan is an implant at 15 Bounty Tokens. So is the whole work-passive-adjacent set. Do not breed for anything you can buy — spend bounty tokens on Artisan, breed for Demon’s Hand and Remarkable Craftsmanship (which you cannot buy), and use the Pal Reverser to fill the last slot.
