The thing to know before you start breeding

Work-speed passives are universal — they apply to all twelve jobs equally, so one template covers every base Pal you will ever breed. The exception is Farming: it is the only job with passives that raise its work suitability level, and it is also the only job that needs them.

So the breeding problem is small: four templates cover the whole game — a general worker, a ranch worker, a combat Pal, and a mount. Everything else is just which Pal you stamp the template onto, because the job is decided by work suitability level, not by passives.

The stacking rule for everything on this page

Unless a passive or partner skill says otherwise, it stacks. And where the text does say "does not stack", that means it will not stack with another of the same species — it still combines freely with different Pals carrying different effects.

So a party can layer Solenne’s +80% player Attack, Robinquill’s +35% bow damage, and a Vanguard passive on every member at +10% each, all at once. Only duplicates are wasted.

Max throughput — +245%

Demon’s Hand + Remarkable Craftsmanship + Artisan + Work Slave. The ceiling. Feed it well and keep a Hot Spring nearby, because Demon’s Hand burns sanity.

Round-the-clock — +215%

Swap Work Slave for Insomnia on any non-nocturnal Pal. You lose 30% speed but gain the whole night, which is a net win on anything running unattended.

Low-maintenance

Hermit Sage + Artisan + Serious. Less peak speed, far less micromanagement — the sanity and hunger drain stops forcing you back to base.

Never put this on a base Pal

Musclehead is −50% Work Speed. It is a combat passive (+30% Attack) and it is also sold as an implant at the Bounty Officer, so it is easy to apply to the wrong Pal by accident. Check before you spend the token.

Sanity is a food problem, not a passive problem

Demon’s Hand costs you SAN, but the fix is quality food and Hot Springs — not dropping the passive. Giving up 90% work speed to save a sanity slot is the wrong trade on a base you visit regularly. Hermit Sage is the right answer only for bases you genuinely leave alone for long stretches.

3b. Passives that buff you, not the Pal

A separate category, and the one most easily missed: these fire from a Pal sitting in the party undeployed, and they stack across party members.

Why this category exists at all

Any Pal you carry but never send out has inert Attack, Defence, HP, Work Speed and movement — those numbers only apply to a deployed Pal. The eight passives below are the entire set that still does something from the bench. On a party built around partner skills rather than Pal combat — see the World Tree farming team — they are the only sensible thing to put in those slots.

The corollary, and the tooltip trap inside it

On the bench, if the effect does not reach the Player, it does nothing — a Pal that never deploys is never hit, never regenerates, never poisoned. Diamond Body, Demon God, Legend and every World Tree attack passive are dead there.

But read the real tooltip before applying that rule. Several passives are dual and say so in game while published tier lists render them as Pal-only. Idiosyncratic is the caught example: its tooltip begins “Pal and Player”, so its +50% Auto Health Regeneration and +25% Defence reach you and only the Poison and Burn immunities are carrier-only. That makes it the best player-Defence passive in the game, and a bench passive rather than a mount one.

Checked, and the answer is no. Immortality reads “Pal Auto Health Regeneration Rate” and its Attack line carries no Player; Heavily Armored is Pal-only explosion immunity. Idiosyncratic is the only dual passive in the Rainbow tier, so the bench loadout stands. The convention is reliable: the game writes Pal, Player, or Pal and Player, and an unqualified stat on a passive means the Pal.

4. World Tree passives

The top tier. Most are a straight trade rather than a free upgrade — read the downside.

They all share one hidden property

Any Pal carrying a World Tree passive stops World Tree resources despawning as you approach — permanently removing the need to drink Holy Water to mine Paloxite.

It belongs to the whole tier rather than one skill, and no description mentions it. So choose by the Pal’s job and take the immunity as a freebie.

You do not have to breed any of these

All seven are in the Ancient Relic Recycler’s common implant pool, from relics of any grade — confirmed in play. Recycle relics, install the Disposable Implant at a Pal Surgery Table, done. See how to get these onto a Pal.

The hunger passive is World Tree’s Bounty — confirmed in play. Earlier builds of this guide called it World Tree Seedbed, which was wrong.

Pick by job, not by the immunity

Every passive in this tier grants the immunity, so pick by the Pal’s job. But the carrier must be deployed — confirmed in play; a benched party Pal does not project it. That means the immunity always costs you a slot on the Pal you actually have out, which on a farming trip is the mount. Given the slot is spent either way, take the one passive in the tier a platform mount can use: Dimensional Leap, +50% movement. Its hunger penalty is answered by carrying food; movement you did not take cannot be bought back.

5. Rainbow passives

From mutated eggs — or, far more reliably, from Glistening Ancient Relics. Several have no downside at all.

Correction — mutated eggs are not the only source

Five of these are the Glistening-relic implant pool in the Ancient Relic Recycler, at 20% each: Immortality, Heavily Armored, Babysitter, Skymarcher, Idiosyncratic. Hard-mode expeditions supply Glistening relics and so does the Wistella route, which drops all five tiers — making Immortality a farm rather than a lottery. Ranch Master and Lavish Hospitality are not in that pool and still need the egg route.

6. Ranch Pals — the one job with its own passives

Farming is the odd one out, in both directions: it has the lowest natural cap of any job, and it is the only job with passives that raise its suitability level.

PassiveEffectRank
Ranch MasterFarming’s Work Suitability +2Rank 4 (diamond)

These are the only two passives in 1.0 that directly raise a work suitability, and both are Farming-only. Nothing equivalent exists for Handiwork, Mining, Kindling or any other job. Confirmed in-game.

Why Farming and nothing else

Most jobs cap at Farming 4 naturally, against 8 for the other suitabilities and 7 for Transporting. Farming starts four levels behind everything else, which is exactly why it gets dedicated passives and the others do not.

Stacking a ranch Pal to the ceiling

  1. Natural cap — Farming 4Where every ranch Pal starts.
  2. +2 Ranch Master → 6Rank-4 passive. The single biggest jump available.
  3. +1 Farmhand → 7Rank-3 passive. Two of your four slots are now spent, which is the real cost of this build.
  4. +1 Applied Ranching Handbook → 8300 Dog Coin at the Medal Merchant. Permanent, and it follows the Pal between bases. 1.0 added this one specifically for Farming.
  5. +1 Cinnamoth work aura → 9Station a Cinnamoth at the base: its partner skill gives +1 Farming to every other Pal there. Does not stack with duplicates, does not benefit itself, and cannot be improved by condensation. There are 12 such aura Pals, one per job — Ribbuny does the same for Handiwork.
  6. Condensation → 10Each rank-up raises one suitability; max rank raises them all. 48 Pals to max rank.
The ranch template

Ranch Master + Farmhand + two work-speed passives. Work speed raises ranch output, not just animation rate, so the last two slots want Artisan (buyable, 15 Bounty Tokens) plus Demon’s Hand, Remarkable Craftsmanship or Work Slave.

Both multipliers are real and independent: suitability level from the two Farming passives, and output from work speed. Plus condensation rank raising drop quantity per cycle on top.

Why this compounds faster than it looks

Your breeding programme is gated by Cake, Cake by Honey, and Honey has no vendor at any price — it comes off a Beegarde ranch and nowhere else. So a maxed Beegarde directly accelerates the pipeline that produces your passives.

Same for Sibelyx Primo (High Quality Cloth), Dumud (HQ Pal Oil → Polymer) and Flambelle (Flame Organ → Carbon Fiber). Those four ranch slots feed most of the endgame.

How to actually get these onto a Pal

  1. BreedingThe main route. Both parents pass passives down, so build up a stock of donor Pals carrying single good passives, then combine. Condense first, breed second — condensation rank is not inherited.
  2. Implants — the shortcutBuyable, no breeding required, and split across two currencies with no overlap. Bounty Officer, 15 tokens each: Artisan, Musclehead, Motivational Leader, Stronghold Strategist, Vanguard, Burly Body, Wellness Watcher. Arena, 50 Battle Tickets each: Serenity, Runner, Ace Swimmer, Infinite Stamina, Noble, Reload Master, Healing Coach.
  3. Pal Surgery TableTech 38. Installs implants, and transfers passives between Pals. Not every passive is transferable — check before you plan a build around it.
  4. Ancient Relic Recycler — the endgame routeTech 74, needs electricity. Converts Ancient Relics into Disposable Implants, installed at the Pal Surgery Table. All seven World Tree passives are in its common pool from relics of any grade (6.25% each of 16), alongside Swift, Demon God, Diamond Body, Remarkable Craftsmanship, Eternal Engine, King of the Waves, Vampiric, Mastery of Fasting and Heart of the Immovable King. Glistening relics have their own pool (20% each of 5): Immortality, Heavily Armored, Babysitter, Skymarcher, Idiosyncratic. Hard-mode expeditions supply Glistening relics, but so does the Wistella route, which drops all five tiers — see farming Ancient Relics. Those shares are within the implant pool, not per relic — roughly one relic in five rolls an implant at all.
  5. Pal ReverserRerolls passives. 7 Bounty Tokens, or 35 Battle Tickets. Cheap enough to fish for a fourth slot.
  6. Mutated eggsA source of Rainbow passives, not the only one — and not breedable on demand. Glistening relics are the controllable route for all but Ranch Master and Lavish Hospitality.
The efficient order

Artisan is an implant at 15 Bounty Tokens. So is the whole work-passive-adjacent set. Do not breed for anything you can buy — spend bounty tokens on Artisan, breed for Demon’s Hand and Remarkable Craftsmanship (which you cannot buy), and use the Pal Reverser to fill the last slot.

7. Talents (IVs) — and why maxing them is a purchase, not a breeding project

Three hidden values per Pal — HP, Attack, Defence — each on a 0–100 scale worth up to +30% of that stat. They are applied before passive bonuses, and they are locked at the moment a Pal is caught or hatched.

Buy the fruit. Breed only to lower the bill.

Guides routinely call IVs “the one layer you cannot buy or grind after the fact”. In 1.0 that is false. Life, Power and Stout Fruit each add +10 talent to HP, Attack and Defence respectively, and the cap is 100 — so ten fruits finish any stat from zero. The Bounty Officer sells all three at 25 tokens.

This guide already listed the fruit and missed what it was for

The merchants page has had Life / Power / Stout Fruit at 25 Bounty Tokens since the first build, filed under “stat fruit”. What was never said is that those are the IV item. Same shape as the Recycler: the expensive-looking problem was already solved by something sitting on a shelf.

The non-obvious bit: only crossing a multiple of ten saves you anything

A fruit is a flat +10 and the cap is 100, so what you actually pay is ⌈(100 − talent) ÷ 10⌉ fruits. A Pal at 71 and a Pal at 79 cost exactly the same to finish — three fruits either way, with the overshoot clipped against the cap.

So breeding a 71 up to a 79 is worth nothing. Breeding it to 81 saves one fruit, or 25 tokens. That single fact decides when to stop hatching: keep breeding only while you expect to cross a ten-boundary, and price each crossing at 25 tokens. Very few breeding sessions are worth 25 tokens an hour. Assumes overshoot is clipped at 100 rather than refused — either way the fruit count is identical.

How inheritance actually works

Worth knowing mainly so you can judge how far breeding will realistically get you.

  • At least one of the three talents is always inherited from a parent — which stat, and from which parent, is random. Game8 established this over a 107-egg test.
  • Each remaining talent is roughly 30% from a given parent and 40% a fresh random roll across the full 0–100 range.
  • So a child can inherit all three from its parents, and good parents ratchet — you cannot go backwards if you always breed your best and keep the winner.
  • Alpha and Legendary Pals tend to roll higher talents, which makes a field boss a better starting parent than a wild catch.
Which cake — and it is not the one you use for passives
  • Mushroom Cake (Tech 30) — improves the offspring’s talent roll. This is the IV cake, and it is cheap: Flour, two kinds of Mushroom, Egg and Honey.
  • Extravagant Vegetable Cake (Tech 60) — adds talent points and raises the mutation chance to roughly 3%. Needs no Honey at all, which is usually the binding constraint.
  • Vegetable Cake — two eggs per bake. Straight throughput against quality; take it while you are still far from a ten-boundary.
  • Special Cake (Tech 74) — passive inheritance, not talents. Different job, different session.

Full recipes are on the demand page. One base with nine ranch slots and a wheat plantation supplies all five.

Reading them

Ability Glasses, unlocked at Level 34, show HP / Attack / Defence talents directly on wild and owned Pals, with 70 and above highlighted green. Without them you can recover the values by entering species, level and the stats shown on the Pal Condensation screen into a community IV calculator — but at Lv80 the glasses are free and instant, so wear them while you catch.

Where this is not worth spending anything

Talents do nothing for a Pal that never deploys. The four bench Pals on the World Tree team — Solenne, both Menastings, and your carry-weight Pal — contribute only partner skills and player-side passives, and their own HP, Attack and Defence are inert. Do not feed them fruit. Those tokens buy Vanguard and Stronghold Strategist implants at 15 instead.

On that team the mount is the only Pal whose own stats matter — and it matters more than this guide first assumed, because it kills with its own abilities. Combat Pals and raid tanks are the other place fruit earns its price.

The order that actually saves time

Breed for passives, not for talents. Passives are what a hatch decides and you cannot cheaply undo; talents are a receipt you settle afterwards. Hatch until the passives are right, read the talents with Ability Glasses, then buy the difference in fruit — and go back to the breeding pen only if a stat sits below about 60, where the bill runs past four fruit for that stat alone.
