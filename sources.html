The single biggest risk in this guide

Most Palworld content on the open web is from the 0.1.x early-access era (January 2024). Recipes, tiers, prices and the entire work-suitability system changed at 1.0. Several sites serve stale early-access data under a 2026 date stamp — I hit at least one that listed Lifmunk, Digtoise, Tanzee and Foxparks as the best base Pals on a page dated after the 1.0 launch. Where two sources conflicted, I preferred the one that named 1.0-specific Pals, structures or patch numbers.

How the merchant data was established

The vendor tables are the strongest part of this guide. They come from two datamine-driven sites — palworld.tools (stamped "Palworld 1.0, build 24088745, updated 13 July 2026") and palmods.gg ("1,891 normalized 1.0 item records"). They independently reference the same internal shop table IDs (Wander_Shop_1, Desert_Shop_1, Volcano_Shop_1, Caravan_Shop_1..25), which is strong mutual corroboration that both are reading real game files rather than paraphrasing each other.

Critically, palmods prints an explicit negative string — "Not recorded — No vendor listing in the current shop tables" — per item. That is what makes the "cannot be bought" verdicts trustworthy rather than an argument from silence.

The caveat: that snapshot is 1.0.0/1.0.1-era. Patches 1.0.2 and 1.0.3 landed afterwards, and 1.0.3 explicitly rebalanced Holy Water sources and Aquatic Construction Kit costs. Nothing in the patch notes suggests shop stock was rewritten, but treat exact gold figures as roughly one patch stale.

Correction log

An earlier version of this guide claimed the endgame bottleneck was the AI Core, on the reasoning that a Beam Launcher unpacks to ~20 Ancient Civilization Cores. A reader running expeditions continuously pointed out that cores accrue passively, which is correct — and following that through changed the conclusion. Once expeditions are running and mining routes are set, every raw input is a couple of evenings of routes; the binding constraint is Ancient Furnace throughput, because a furnace takes only two Pals and splits its workload across two different suitabilities. The advice now points at building more furnaces rather than farming more ore.

Second correction, 4 Sep: an earlier version stated flatly that different Pals with the same drop-boost effect do stack. A second 1.0 source said the opposite, so the claim was withdrawn and marked unresolved. The same pass replaced a speculative "Rainbow passive" note about Paloxite with the better-sourced World Tree passive (Dimensional Leap / Hermit Sage), which grants permanent protection.

Resolved, same day: a player report from live 1.0.3 play settled it — "does not stack" applies to the same species, and different species stack, with regional variants counting as different species (Menasting + Menasting Terra). Both published sources were correct and describing the same rule from opposite ends; KeenGamer’s "multiple copies" meant duplicates of one species. The rule is now stated positively on the farming page. Worth noting for future work: two sources appearing to contradict each other is sometimes a vocabulary problem, not a factual one.

Fifth correction, 5 Sep: this guide called Paloxite "the real bottleneck", reasoning that it came only from three named clusters (~12 nodes) while Holy Water had many sources. A map screenshot plus the player’s own experience shows ~76 nodes region-wide. Paloxite is plentiful and the constraint returns to Ancient Furnace throughput. The lesson is the same one the Sunreach route taught: published guides name a few convenient spots near fast-travel points, and reading that as an inventory rather than a sample produced a wrong conclusion twice.

Sixth correction, 5 Sep: this guide told the reader to breed Dimensional Leap or Hermit Sage specifically, for the Paloxite immunity. Every World Tree passive grants it — it belongs to the tier, and no skill description says so. The advice was needlessly constraining: pick the passive that suits the Pal’s job and the immunity is free. Note the shape of this one: I inferred a rule from the two examples I happened to have, rather than asking what the examples had in common.

Third correction, 4 Sep: this site claimed there are no per-job passives. In-game screenshots show Farmhand (Farming’s Work Suitability +1) and Ranch Master (+2) — so per-job passives do exist, they are just confined to Farming, which is also the only job with a natural cap of 4 instead of 8. The claim now reads "work-speed passives are universal; Farming is the one job with suitability passives". The generalisation was drawn from an absence of evidence about other jobs, which is exactly the move this guide warns about elsewhere.

Fourth correction, 4 Sep: the guide treated two published Plasma Rifle recipes as a conflict and picked one. An in-game screenshot shows a third set of numbers, and the reason all three exist is that weapon material cost scales with schematic rarity. There was never a contradiction — only an unstated variable. Every weapon row in the guide is now labelled as a snapshot of one unknown rung. Second time a "source conflict" turned out to be a missing distinction rather than an error; worth suspecting that first.

Two things worth correcting if you have read them elsewhere

  • "World Tree passives must be bred" — including in earlier builds of this guide. The Ancient Relic Recycler (Tech 74) puts all seven in its common implant pool, from relics of any grade. Likewise "mutated eggs are the only source of Rainbow passives": Glistening relics yield Immortality, Heavily Armored, Babysitter, Skymarcher and Idiosyncratic at 20% each.
  • "Genetic Research Module" — no item by that name appears in 1.0 data. The implant system uses Implant: <passive> items (15 Bounty Tokens or 50 Battle Tickets) plus the Pal Reverser. If you have seen the term, it is probably a misremembered name.
  • "Mysterious Mushroom" — likewise not found in 1.0 data. Only plain Mushroom and Cavern Mushroom exist, both from a Shroomer ranch.
  • Paldium at 20g — that is an early-access price. It is 70g in 1.0.
