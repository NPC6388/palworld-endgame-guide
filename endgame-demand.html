If your expeditions are already running, your ceiling is base worker slots

Raw ore is cheap once the loops are set — a single Sunreach run yields more Soralite than a Beam Launcher needs. Ancient Furnaces have no practical build limit, so ingot throughput is not capped by the building either. What caps it is that each furnace consumes two of your base’s worker slots, and a base holds 15 workers on default settings.

And once the weapons are built, the only cost that keeps recurring is ammunition.

The furnace maths

The Ancient Furnace requires Kindling 6 and Cooling 6, and its refinement workload is split evenly between those two suitabilities — so a Renjishi (Kindling 8) working alone does nothing for the Cooling half. Pair it with Bastigor (Cooling 8).

Two Pals per furnace, and you can build as many furnaces as you like. So the arithmetic is simply worker slots ÷ 2:

  • 15 workers per base (the default world setting) = 7 furnaces, if you dedicate an entire base to smelting and nothing else.
  • The worker cap actually climbs +1 per base level up to 30, but the default world setting holds it at 15. Raise Base worker limit in world settings and the full ladder applies — vanilla allows up to 50.
  • Base slots themselves unlock through Base Missions (Base Level cap 35), so more bases is the other axis.

The practical move: a dedicated smelting base. Ore and quartz arrive by hand anyway, so it does not need miners — just furnace pairs, a transporter or two, and storage.

On the "workload" numbers

Workload is how long a Pal takes to produce the item. Confirmed in-game: Computer 2,500, AI Core 50,000.

An earlier version of this page quoted 700,000 for a Soralite Ingot and 1,000,000 for a Paloxite Ingot from a datamine. Those figures cannot be right — an AI Core consumes ten Soralite Ingots and is itself only 50,000. They have been removed rather than left to mislead.

In any case the absolute numbers are close to moot once you run the best available Pals with bred work-speed passives — a +245% work stack on Kindling/Cooling 10 workers moves the real rate far more. Use workload to decide what to batch, not to predict wall-clock time.

Worked example — one Beam Launcher

The Beam Launcher (Tech 80) costs 100 Paloxite Ingot + 50 Bio Battery + 10 AI Core + 10 Ancient Civilization Core. Unpacked:

  • 100 Paloxite Ingot = 200 Paloxite + 100 Soralite + 100 World Tree Holy Water
  • 10 AI Core = 50 Computer + 100 Soralite Ingot + 20 Thermal Core + 10 Ancient Civilization Core
  • 50 Computer = 100 Circuit Board + 150 Plasteel + 100 Bio Battery + 100 Carbon Fiber
  • 150 Plasteel = 750 Crude Oil + 750 Paldium + 1,500 Ore
  • 100 Circuit Board = 200 Pure Quartz + 100 Polymer
  • 20 Thermal Core = 80 Flame Organ + 160 Coal + 40 Corrosive Solvent + 40 Hexolite
  • 100 Soralite Ingot (for the AI Cores alone) = 200 Soralite + 200 Pure Quartz

So a single Beam Launcher needs roughly 20 Ancient Civilization Cores, 300 Soralite, 200 Paloxite, 40 Hexolite and 100 Holy Water — before ammunition. Derived arithmetic from the recipes below; treat as an order-of-magnitude figure.

Now put those numbers in context

Every one of those figures is small against an established farming loop, which is why the furnace is the real constraint:

  • 300 Soralite — a full Sunreach loop returns 1,200–1,600 in 10–12 minutes, respawning every 30 minutes. That is a quarter of one run.
  • 200 Paloxite — "stacks of hundreds per run" once the route is set. Roughly one trip.
  • 20 Ancient Civilization Cores — Sunreach Isle expeditions return 3–4 per hour per station, and stations stack across bases. If you run expeditions continuously this accrues in the background and you will likely never notice it.
  • 40 Hexolite — one Feybreak trip, shared with the Chromite run.
  • 1,500 Ore, 750 Crude Oil, 160 Coal, 400 Pure Quartz, 80 Flame Organ, 50 Electric Organ — all fully automatable at base. The 1,500 Ore for Plasteel is the figure that surprises people: it is why a serious ore base still earns its keep at Lv80.

The materials are a couple of evenings of routes. The 100 Paloxite Ingots and 100 Soralite Ingots those materials become are what you will actually wait on.

Ammunition — the only cost that recurs

A weapon is a one-off. Ammo is the bill you pay forever, so this is the table that decides what you actually carry.

The dividing line is Paloxite

Every ammo type up to Tech 72 costs zero Paloxite and zero Holy Water — it runs entirely on Charcoal, Sulfur, Soralite and Quartz, all of which are base-automatable or a 12-minute Sunreach loop. Everything from Tech 75 up burns Paloxite, which means a World Tree trip and the 30-second Holy Water scramble for every magazine.

What this means in trips

Paloxite is the only input here that costs you a gated field trip — World Tree only, Plasma Multicutter required, and every node behind a 30-second buff window. Take a run that nets 200 Paloxite:

  • Beam Launcher — about 165 rounds
  • Beam Scatter — about 330 rounds
  • Plasma Rifle — about 665 rounds
  • Heavy Assault Rifle, SMG, Shotgun, Grenade Launcher, Bow — unlimited. They do not touch Paloxite at all.
The practical loadout
  • Mechanical Bow for single targets. 20,000 Attack, 0.4 Soralite per round, no Paloxite, Tech 67. Slow but precise, and it rewards headshots — the pick for alphas and picking off high-value spawns, not for a crowd.
  • Beam Launcher when you need burst. 14,000 Attack and the best damage per Paloxite in the game — not the extravagance this guide first called it.
  • Heavy Assault Rifle or Combat SMG for volume fire. Zero Paloxite and fully automatable inputs.
  • Terraprisma for sustained farming. 4,050 DPS at Common and no ammunition at all. You spend durability instead of materials — and a Legendary schematic doubles that durability to 10,368 while lifting DPS to 6,750.
  • Plasma Rifle only for its chaining. On raw efficiency it is the weakest of the four comparable weapons.

The weapon I had missed: Tactical Grenade Launcher

6,722 Attack at Common, and its ammunition uses no Paloxite at all — 10 Gunpowder + 4 Soralite Ingot per 10 rounds. That is 4.2× the damage of the Heavy Assault Rifle for materials in the same automatable class.

The catch is durability 800 at Common, the lowest of any endgame weapon — roughly a seventh of the Heavy Assault Rifle’s. It is a weapon you will recraft often. But since it costs no Paloxite, recrafting is cheap.

Where that leaves the ranking
  • Mechanical Bow wins outright on materials — 50,000 Attack per Soralite, zero Paloxite. Slow but precise and headshot-friendly, so it converts best on single high-value targets.
  • Beam Launcher is the best Paloxite spend — 11,667 damage per Paloxite against the Plasma Rifle’s 6,200. Worth carrying for burst.
  • Tactical Grenade Launcher is the best free damage — 4.2× a Heavy Assault Rifle round, no Paloxite. Just expect to rebuild it.
  • Plasma Rifle is the durability king — 25,000 Common, 100,000 Legendary, far beyond anything else. Its case is that it almost never needs replacing, not its damage.
  • Terraprisma for sustain — 4,050 DPS, no ammunition, and a Legendary schematic doubles durability to 10,368 while lifting DPS to 6,750.
  • Beam Scatter is the weakest — same ammo materials as the Plasma Rifle at half the yield, for 508 per pellet. Only justified if the pellet count is high.

Rarity scales cost as well as stats — correction

I withdrew this theory earlier. The full database reinstates it.

Per-rarity recipes climb steadily. The Beam Launcher runs 100 → 125 → 150 → 175 → 200 Paloxite Ingot from Common to Legendary, with AI Core going 10 → 20 and Ancient Civilization Core 10 → 20. Every weapon behaves the same way, and higher rarities also add Ancient Civilization Parts that the Common recipe does not require at all.

I had withdrawn the rarity-scales-cost idea when three technology panels matched published figures exactly. Those panels show the Common recipe, and the published figures were Common — so they agreed for a reason that was not evidence against scaling. The theory was right; my test of it was not.

On your own save: your Plasma Rifle panel reads 68 Paloxite Ingot / 42 Bio Battery / 6 AI Core / 5 Ancient Civ Core where the standard Common recipe is 80 / 50 / 8 / 7 — consistently about 15% lower. Something on your save is reducing crafting cost. Worth identifying, because it applies to everything.

Durability is where rarity really pays

Attack rises only 1.2–1.67× from Common to Legendary, but durability rises 4× across the board — Plasma Rifle 25,000 → 100,000, Combat SMG 8,000 → 32,000, Drone Launcher 6,500 → 26,000, Mechanical Bow 2,000 → 8,000.

So upgrading a schematic is mostly buying uptime, not damage. For the weapons whose only real cost is wear — Terraprisma, and the low-durability Tactical Grenade Launcher — that is the whole value proposition.

What this implies for the Mechanical Bow

The Advanced Bow fires every 1.667s — the archetype reason bows feel slow, exactly as you found. At 8,700 Attack that gives 5,219 DPS.

If the Mechanical Bow shares that cadence, its 20,000 Attack implies roughly 12,000 DPS before headshot multipliers — second only to the Overheat Rifle swap trick. Archetype inference, not measurement: no 1.0 endgame weapon has published shot-interval data.

Which raid to farm

Bellanoir Libero and Hartalis return their Ultra Slab at 100% — they are infinitely repeatable. Blazamut Ryu and Xenolord return it at only 10%, so each run is very likely to cost you the slab. Farm the first two for Ancient Civilization Cores; treat the other two as one-offs for the cosmetic and the egg.

Hartalis is also the softest fight — 80% damage reduction against 91% on the others, and roughly 400,000 less HP.

All Ultra raids also drop Applied Handbooks at 33.3% per variant, which is a second source alongside the 300-Dog-Coin Medal Merchant.

Your stat spread at Lv80

Roughly 79 status points across a full playthrough, and each stat caps at 50 invested points.

  1. Weight — 50 points (max it)First priority, no debate. Every farming loop in this guide is gated on how much ore, ingot and Holy Water you can carry home.
  2. Stamina — the bulk of the remaining 29The World Tree, Sunreach and the Sky Islands are all long glides and vertical climbs. This is a 1.0-specific priority shift — the old islands did not demand it.
  3. HP — whatever is leftA buffer for raids and Alphas.
  4. Attack and Work Speed — skip bothYour Pals outperform you in both roles. Weapon tier, ammo type and your active Pals’ skills swing damage far more than the per-point Attack bump.

  • Jetragon — tech unlock 79 → 70; recipe simplified to 48 Soralite Ingot + 12 AI Core from 60 Paloxite Ingot + 24 AI Core, dropping the World Tree requirement entirely.

  • Oil infrastructure — build costs down, extraction speed up.
