Run Dusty Ravine, not Alluvion Lakefront

-1560, 1510 is the only spot that pays both ways: its Holy Water droppers — Orserk and Snock Lux — are both Electric, so a single booster pair covers both species, and the tightest Paloxite cluster in the game sits right on the fast-travel point.

Alluvion Lakefront has the higher raw Holy Water rate, but its droppers are Starryon Primo (Neutral) and Shaolong (Water) — two elements, so three of your five slots go to boosters, and there is no Paloxite there at all. Dusty Ravine gets both resources from one loop with two booster slots.

Correction — Paloxite is not the bottleneck I called it

This page previously said "Paloxite is your real bottleneck, and no Pal can help with it", reasoning that Holy Water has many sources while Paloxite comes only from three named clusters.

That was built on an undercount. The published guides name three clusters totalling about twelve nodes; the map below shows roughly seventy-six across the region, and that is confirmed by firsthand play. One lap of the circuit visits 61 of them.

So Paloxite is plentiful. Neither half of the Paloxite Ingot recipe is scarce — which puts the constraint back where the rest of this guide already had it: Ancient Furnace throughput, capped by base worker slots. You will gather ore faster than two Pals per furnace can smelt it.

What still holds

Drop boosters genuinely do nothing for mining — that part was right. They multiply items from defeated Pals only, so on this trip they are pure Holy Water value while your tool does the Paloxite work. And the ratio is unchanged: a Paloxite Ingot takes 2 Paloxite + 1 Holy Water, so gather roughly twice as much ore as water.

Which World Tree passive for a mount that also kills

Corrected: the mount is not just a firing platform. It uses its own abilities to kill, so its Attack is live and most of the tier is back in contention.

Two damage pipelines, and they do not feed each other

Worth being explicit, because it decides every slot on this page. Vanguard and Solenne buff Player Attack — your gun. Demon God, Legend and the World Tree attack passives buff the Pal’s Attack — the mount’s abilities. Neither touches the other.

But they are funded from separate, non-fungible pools, which makes this far less of a dilemma than it sounds. The sixteen bench passive slots can only buy player damage — there is nothing Pal-side to put in them. The mount’s four passive slots can only buy Pal damage. Neither pool trades against the other, so you max both, and no choice is involved in the great majority of the investment.

The pipelines compete in exactly one place: the five party slots, where Solenne’s +80% player Attack sits against a drop booster or a utility Pal. That single decision is the only one worth thinking about — and it is decided by measurement, not by picking a side. See the threshold test.

Resolved — the immunity only works on the deployed Pal

Confirmed in play: a World Tree passive sitting on a benched party Pal does not stop resources despawning. The carrier has to be out. On this team the only Pal ever deployed is the mount, so the mount’s World Tree slot is mandatory, not a choice — and that collapses the table above.

One of the four mount slots is spent on the tier regardless, so the real question is which stat that slot buys. Two candidates, and they are the largest single-slot numbers in the game for their stat:

  • Dimensional Leap — +50% movement. Nothing else in the game gives more than +30% (Swift). Its hunger penalty is zero on this save, since a feedbag covers it.
  • Twin-Edged Holy Blade — +50% Attack, −30% Defence. Nothing else gives more than +30% (Demon God, Musclehead).

Take Dimensional Leap. Not because attack does not matter now, but because the rest of the slot pool is asymmetric: outside this tier you can buy attack up to +30% a slot and cooldown reduction on top, while movement outside the tier caps at Swift’s +30%. Spend the scarce slot on the stat you cannot buy elsewhere, and fund attack from the three ordinary slots.

On Eidrolon Ignis specifically

Dragon/Fire, with a 2,750 riding sprint speed that rivals Jetragon. On a 61-node circuit traversal is most of the run, so that alone justifies the pick.

Type-wise it lands better than you might expect. Mounted, your attacks take its element, and Dragon is advantaged into Orserk (Dragon/Electric). Against Snock Lux (Electric) it is neutral, where a Ground mount would be super-effective — so you trade some damage on half the targets for speed across the whole lap.

Do not expect much from its partner skill. Resentful Pterosaur gives +2/3/4/5/6% Attack and Movement per other Dragon or Fire Pal in the party. Your boosters and utility Pals are neither, so here it contributes close to nothing. It shines in a Dragon/Fire team, not this one — ride it for the sprint speed.

The decision rule: durability is spent per hit, not per damage

Weapons lose 1 durability per attack regardless of how hard that attack lands. So durability efficiency is simply damage-per-hit, and it splits the arsenal cleanly:

  • Mechanical Bow — 20,000 damage per durability point. Its 2,000 durability is 2,000 shots. Durability is a non-issue; skip Tetroise Primo.
  • Tactical Grenade Launcher — 6,722 per point. Also fine, despite the alarming 800 figure.
  • Drone Launcher and Terraprisma — 200 and 90 per hit, from nine units attacking constantly. These chew durability faster than anything else in the game. Tetroise Primo is close to mandatory.

And note weapons at zero durability are repaired, not replaced — the cost is maintenance, not a rebuild. That makes low-durability weapons far less punishing than their numbers suggest.

Two loadouts

Bow or Grenade Launcher — max drops

Menasting · Menasting Terra · Solenne + Cryolinx + mount.

+160% drops and +80% player Attack. Durability looks after itself at 20,000 damage a hit. This is the default.

Drone Launcher — zero recurring cost

Menasting · Solenne · Tetroise Primo + Cryolinx + mount.

Drops fall to +80%, but the Drone Launcher uses no ammunition at all and Tetroise Primo removes its one weakness. Nothing about this loadout consumes materials — ideal when you are farming Paloxite and refuse to spend any on ammo.

Check whether you are already one-shotting before you take Solenne

Damage boosters only convert into drops if kills are damage-limited. With a 20,000-Attack Bow and headshots against Lv70–80 targets you may well be one-shotting already, in which case Solenne’s +80% buys nothing and that slot belongs to Cryolinx instead. Total yield is drops per kill × kills per hour, so the right answer depends on which term you are actually short on. The threshold is 24% — Solenne has to cut clear time by more than that to beat Cryolinx’s 1.31× on Orserk. The two-lap test settles it, and note that one-shotting is not all-or-nothing here: you are dealing part of the damage and the mount is dealing the rest, so what you are really measuring is which half is short.

Player damage: the part I had wrong

Correction — booster passives are not irrelevant after all

An earlier version of this page said drop boosters "never deploy, so their passives are irrelevant". That is wrong for a mounted player. A whole class of passives buffs you from the party bench, and they apply no matter what the carrier is doing.

Vanguard: +10% player Attack, and it stacks additively across every Pal in the party carrying it. Five Vanguards is +50% player Attack. Stacking reported by one source; the effect itself is well documented.

It is buyable. Vanguard is a Bounty Officer implant at 15 tokens. Five of them is 75 Bounty Tokens and no breeding at all — comfortably the cheapest damage upgrade in this build. Stronghold Strategist (player defence) is on the same shop shelf if you want a survivability slot.

The other three slots

Vanguard was only the first of four. The four bench Pals hold 16 passive slots between them — and there are only eight passives in the game that do anything at all from the bench.

The filter that decides every one of those slots

Mine Foreman is what this page was missing

The whole trip is a 61-node mining lap, and Mine Foreman raises your own pickaxe efficiency by 25% — it is not a base work passive. On four bench Pals that is +100% mining efficiency, which attacks the half of the route that Vanguard, Solenne and the Menastings all leave completely untouched.

Everything on this page so far has optimised kills. Total yield is drops per kill × kills per hour, but Paloxite does not come from kills at all — it comes from swinging a Plasma Multicutter at rocks. Mine Foreman is the only passive in the game that makes that faster, and it stacks the same way Vanguard does. Stacking across party members is reported for this class of passive rather than measured — same footing as the Vanguard stacking claim above.

Correction — Idiosyncratic does reach the player

The rule is right — on the bench, only what reaches the player does anything — but you have to read the real tooltip to apply it, and I did not. I called Idiosyncratic inert on a bench Pal from a guide summary that rendered it “Defence +25%, auto regen +50%, Poison and Burn immunity”. The in-game tooltip begins “Pal and Player”.

So the split is: Auto Health Regeneration +50% and Defence +25% reach you; only the Poison and Burn immunities are Pal-only. That makes Idiosyncratic the strongest player-Defence passive in the game — two and a half times Stronghold Strategist per slot — and it belongs on the bench after all.

What still holds: the immunities are carrier-only, so Idiosyncratic is not gas cover for you. That is the Corrosive Mist Mask’s job. And genuinely Pal-side passives — Diamond Body, Demon God, Legend, the World Tree attack passives — remain worthless on a Pal that never deploys.

Both follow-ups checked from tooltips: Immortality is Pal-only (“Pal Auto Health Regeneration Rate”, and its Attack line carries no Player) and Heavily Armored is Pal-only explosion immunity. Idiosyncratic is the only dual passive in the Rainbow tier, so this loadout is final. The tooltip convention is reliable — the game writes Pal, Player or Pal and Player, and an unqualified stat means the Pal.

Why Logging Foreman comes out despite being free and bigger

Mythical Wood is not scarce, so a +100% multiplier on gathering it is worth nothing. The Recycler drops Mythical Wood at 100% from every relic tier, so a relic route is already a wood route — and expeditions and kills add more on top. Free and large does not beat useful and small.

This is the same test that put Mine Foreman in: a passive is worth a seat only if the thing it multiplies is something you are actually short of. Paloxite is; Mythical Wood is not.

What actually needs breeding

Buy the passives, recycle the rest, condense three

Nothing here is a breeding project at all. Vanguard, Mine Foreman and Stronghold Strategist are implants you buy. A World Tree passive is an implant you recycle. Everything else that matters scales with condensation.

Correction — World Tree passives are not a breeding project any more

This page used to call a World Tree passive “the one thing you genuinely have to breed”. That is wrong. The Ancient Relic Recycler (Tech 74) converts Ancient Relics into Disposable Implants, and all seven World Tree passives are in its common pool — from relics of any grade. Confirmed in play: the player has pulled multiple World Tree passives from it.

Two boosters at ☆0 give +80% — exactly what one booster at ☆4 gives. So simply catching both Menastings gets you to the same place as a full 48-Pal condensation project on one of them, instantly.

Why Eternal Engine came out, having gone in an hour ago

I put it in on the reasoning that flight drains stamina continuously. True in general — but this route lands at 61 nodes, and stamina regenerates on the ground. The legs between clusters are short enough that the tank rarely empties, so the slot is better spent on damage now that the mount is fighting. Keep Eternal Engine if you find yourself gliding down mid-flight; that is the symptom that would justify it.

For the same reason, ignore the earlier suggestion to swap Legend for Runner. That rested on Legend’s Attack and Defence being dead weight, which is no longer true — Legend’s +20% Attack is live, and Runner’s movement-only +20% is now the weaker of the two.

The combat Pal

Immortality (absorbs 100% of damage dealt as healing, +100% regen, +15% Atk) · Demon God (+30% Atk) · Serenity (−30% cooldown, buyable for 50 Battle Tickets) · Legend (+20% all).

The balanced stack rather than the glass cannon — you are grinding a route, not burning a boss, so survivability is throughput.

Both Runner and Serenity are Arena implants at 50 Battle Tickets. Immortality is a Glistening-relic implant and Demon God is in the common pool, so the combat stack is bought and recycled rather than bred — the only thing left to spend real effort on is condensation.

Gear and consumables

  • Plasma Multicutter (Tech 54) — mandatory. A standard pickaxe cannot break Paloxite at all, and the Multicutter also doubles yield per node.

What the 20% you are skipping actually costs

The dropped nodes are not clustered in one corner — they are isolated stragglers. Their median distance to the nearest other node is 46px against 22px for the ones on the circuit, so each costs roughly double the travel to reach and returns the same single node.

The efficiency curve is remarkably flat between about 70% and 85% coverage, so there is no sharp optimum to hit — anywhere in that band is within a couple of percent of best. Your instinct of ~80% sits right in it.

The three named clusters — and why guides only name three

These are worth knowing as fast-travel anchors. They are not the extent of the deposit.

The published guides undercount this badly

Every written guide covering Paloxite names the same three clusters, about twelve nodes in total. The map above has roughly seventy-six, spread across the whole region — and that matches firsthand play rather than only the screenshot.

The pattern is worth carrying into anything else you read: guides highlight a few convenient named spots near fast-travel points, and it is easy to read that as an inventory. It is a sample. The loop

  1. Fast-travel to Dusty Ravine -1560, 1510Three tight Paloxite nodes right at the point. Mine them first, before anything aggros.
  2. Clear the Orserk and Snock Lux spawnsBoth Electric, both boosted by your Menasting pair. 1–3 Holy Water each at a 50% rate.
  3. Run the other two clusters-1710, 1535 Remnant Riverside (3 nodes plus scattered extras on the exit paths), then -1800, 1465 Gilded City Ruins (6 nodes, two trios close enough to chain). See the route map — 12 nodes a lap.
  4. Capture rather than kill, five times per speciesThe capture bonus completes at 5 catches in 1.0, and pays Technology Points plus a permanent stat bonus on future catches of that species. Do it once, then go back to killing.
  5. Fast-travel out and back to resetThen let the Large Fishing Pond top up your Holy Water at base while you are away.
Mind your Pal on the approach

Paloxite crystals despawn from your Pal’s proximity as well as your own. Without World Tree passive protection, a mount running ahead of you will wipe the node before you reach it — which is the single most common way people lose a cluster.
