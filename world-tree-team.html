Run Dusty Ravine, not Alluvion Lakefront

-1560, 1510 is the only spot that pays both ways: its Holy Water droppers — Orserk and Snock Lux — are both Electric, so a single booster pair covers both species, and the tightest Paloxite cluster in the game sits right on the fast-travel point.

Alluvion Lakefront has the higher raw Holy Water rate, but its droppers are Starryon Primo (Neutral) and Shaolong (Water) — two elements, so three of your five slots go to boosters, and there is no Paloxite there at all. Dusty Ravine gets both resources from one loop with two booster slots.

Set your expectations honestly before you build this

Drop boosters do nothing for Paloxite. They only multiply items from defeated Pals, so they are pure Holy Water value — mining is gated by your tool, not your party.

And a Paloxite Ingot costs 2 Paloxite + 1 Holy Water, so you need twice as much Paloxite as Holy Water. Holy Water has many sources (fishing at 43–71 a catch, the Large Fishing Pond generating it passively at base, alpha bosses, expeditions); Paloxite has exactly one — hand-mining these nodes.

So Paloxite is your real bottleneck, and no Pal can help with it. Build this team anyway — it makes the Holy Water half essentially free while you are standing there for the Paloxite — but spend your route time on nodes, not kills.

The five slots

Two are fixed by the region. The other three are a genuine trade, and your weapon decides how to spend them.

Fixed — the World Tree demands these

  1. DandilordMist Stalwart — Poison immunity for you and your party, nullifies the World Tree toxic gas outright, Poison 2 on your attacks. One only.
  2. A Ground-element mount carrying Dimensional Leap+50% movement, permanent immunity to the Paloxite despawn aura, a mounted +5→20% Attack, and — because a mounted partner skill changes your attack type to the mount’s element — Ground damage into two Electric targets. Four jobs, one slot.

The decision rule: durability is spent per hit, not per damage

Weapons lose 1 durability per attack regardless of how hard that attack lands. So durability efficiency is simply damage-per-hit, and it splits the arsenal cleanly:

  • Mechanical Bow — 20,000 damage per durability point. Its 2,000 durability is 2,000 shots. Durability is a non-issue; skip Tetroise Primo.
  • Tactical Grenade Launcher — 6,722 per point. Also fine, despite the alarming 800 figure.
  • Drone Launcher and Terraprisma — 200 and 90 per hit, from nine units attacking constantly. These chew durability faster than anything else in the game. Tetroise Primo is close to mandatory.

And note weapons at zero durability are repaired, not replaced — the cost is maintenance, not a rebuild. That makes low-durability weapons far less punishing than their numbers suggest.

Two loadouts

Bow or Grenade Launcher — max drops

Menasting · Menasting Terra · Solenne + Dandilord + mount.

+160% drops and +80% player Attack. Durability looks after itself at 20,000 damage a hit. This is the default.

Drone Launcher — zero recurring cost

Menasting · Solenne · Tetroise Primo + Dandilord + mount.

Drops fall to +80%, but the Drone Launcher uses no ammunition at all and Tetroise Primo removes its one weakness. Nothing about this loadout consumes materials — ideal when you are farming Paloxite and refuse to spend any on ammo.

Check whether you are already one-shotting before you take Solenne

Damage boosters only convert into drops if kills are damage-limited. With a 20,000-Attack Bow and headshots against Lv70–80 targets you may well be one-shotting already, in which case Solenne’s +80% buys nothing and that slot belongs to Menasting Terra instead. Total yield is drops per kill × kills per hour, so the right answer depends on which term you are actually short on. If the route feels travel-bound rather than fight-bound, take the drops.

Player damage: the part I had wrong

Correction — booster passives are not irrelevant after all

An earlier version of this page said drop boosters "never deploy, so their passives are irrelevant". That is wrong for a mounted player. A whole class of passives buffs you from the party bench, and they apply no matter what the carrier is doing.

Vanguard: +10% player Attack, and it stacks additively across every Pal in the party carrying it. Five Vanguards is +50% player Attack. Stacking reported by one source; the effect itself is well documented.

It is buyable. Vanguard is a Bounty Officer implant at 15 tokens. Five of them is 75 Bounty Tokens and no breeding at all — comfortably the cheapest damage upgrade in this build. Stronghold Strategist (player defence) is on the same shop shelf if you want a survivability slot.

If you are running the Mechanical Bow

You said the Bow is slow but precise and headshot-friendly, which makes Robinquill’s +35% bow damage a real contender for a slot. The trade is one Menasting: you drop from +160% to +80% on Holy Water drops (a 1.44× loss per kill) to gain about 35% kill speed. Those come out close to even, so take it if you prefer fewer, faster fights — and note it only helps the Bow, not the Grenade Launcher.

What actually needs breeding

Condense Solenne before the Menastings

Both projects cost 48 Pals. Solenne’s takes you from +30% to +80% player Attack and pays off in every fight you will ever take — raids, alphas, towers. The Menastings’ takes drops from +40% to +80% and only ever helps you kill Electric Pals. Same cost, much wider benefit.

Note the mount is the only real breeding project — Dimensional Leap is a World Tree passive and cannot be bought. Buy Runner (50 Battle Tickets) rather than breeding it, and spend the effort on Dimensional Leap.

The shortcut worth knowing

Two boosters at ☆0 give +80% — exactly what one booster at ☆4 gives. So simply catching both Menastings gets you to the same place as a full 48-Pal condensation project on one of them, instantly.

Start there. Condense afterwards, at your own pace, for the last +80%. Max rank is 48 Pals per species in 1.0 (down from 116), so a full ☆4 pair is 96 Menastings — worth doing for a farm you will run for weeks, not worth blocking on before your first trip.

Passives for the two Pals that need them

The mount

Dimensional Leap (+50% move, Paloxite immunity) · Swift (+30%) · Legend (+20% all) · Runner (+20%, buyable as an implant for 50 Battle Tickets).

Roughly +120% movement. On this route travel between node clusters dominates, so this slot earns more than it looks.

The combat Pal

Immortality (Life Steal, +100% regen, +15% Atk) · Demon God (+30% Atk) · Serenity (−30% cooldown, buyable for 50 Battle Tickets) · Legend (+20% all).

The balanced stack rather than the glass cannon — you are grinding a route, not burning a boss, so survivability is throughput.

Both Runner and Serenity are Arena implants at 50 Battle Tickets. Buy them rather than breeding them, and spend your breeding effort on Dimensional Leap and Immortality, which you cannot buy.

Gear and consumables

  • Plasma Multicutter (Tech 54) — mandatory. A standard pickaxe cannot break Paloxite at all, and the Multicutter also doubles yield per node.
  • No Holy Water needed for mining once your mount carries Dimensional Leap. Without it, carry hundreds — weight is 0.1 since 1.0.3, and irrelevant on your settings anyway.
  • Ammo: the Tactical Grenade Launcher is the best choice here — 6,722 Attack and its ammunition costs no Paloxite, so you are not spending the resource you came to collect. Do not farm Paloxite with a Beam Launcher.

The loop

  1. Fast-travel to Dusty Ravine -1560, 1510Three tight Paloxite nodes right at the point. Mine them first, before anything aggros.
  2. Clear the Orserk and Snock Lux spawnsBoth Electric, both boosted by your Menasting pair. 1–3 Holy Water each at a 50% rate.
  3. Run the other two clusters-1710, 1535 Remnant Riverside (3 nodes plus scattered extras on the exit paths), then -1800, 1465 Gilded City Ruins (6 nodes, two trios close enough to chain). See the route map — 12 nodes a lap.
  4. Capture rather than kill, five times per speciesThe capture bonus completes at 5 catches in 1.0, and pays Technology Points plus a permanent stat bonus on future catches of that species. Do it once, then go back to killing.
  5. Fast-travel out and back to resetThen let the Large Fishing Pond top up your Holy Water at base while you are away.
Mind your Pal on the approach

Paloxite crystals despawn from your Pal’s proximity as well as your own. Without Dimensional Leap protection, a mount running ahead of you will wipe the node before you reach it — which is the single most common way people lose a cluster.
